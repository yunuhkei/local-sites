# Staying legal (plain English - not legal advice)

- **CAN-SPAM (email):** one-to-one is fine; use your real name, an honest subject, a way to opt out, and honor opt-outs fast. No bought lists, no mass emailing.
- **TCPA (calls/texts):** do **not** use autodialers, prerecorded/robocall voice, or bulk-text software. Personally typing a text to one business is different, but keep it rare, identify yourself, and stop if told to. Check the Do-Not-Call rules and Wisconsin's telemarketing rules (Wisconsin DATCP) before phoning anyone, or ask a local small-business advisor. Walking in is the safest.
- **Data:** leads come from OpenStreetMap (ODbL license, "(c) OpenStreetMap contributors"). Don't scrape sites whose terms forbid it.
- **Demos:** never present a demo as the business's official site. Demos are labeled "preview" and hidden from Google. Don't use a business's logos/photos you don't have rights to.
- **Contracts:** get a short written OK (a text/email is fine) before going live and before charging. Let clients cancel anytime.
- **Taxes:** track income; consider a simple business registration and ask about Wisconsin sales tax on services/website hosting.

## Using the company name instead of your own name (plain English, not legal advice)
- **A business name is legal and normal.** "Cream City Web Co." can be the name on the website, signs, price sheet, texts and cards. Check with the Wisconsin Department of Financial Institutions (DFI) or your county whether a sole proprietor needs to register a trade name (I'm not certain, so confirm).
- **A made-up personal name on marketing is not illegal by itself**, and many people use a professional first name. It becomes a problem if you use it where the other side is relying on who you really are: contracts, invoices and receipts, Stripe, your bank, taxes, domain registration, or any official form. Never claim to be a different real person.
- **Best setup for hiring workers later: form an LLC** (for example "Cream City Web Co., LLC"). The LLC signs the contracts and receives the money, so your own name doesn't need to be on anything customers see. You file with Wisconsin DFI (a fee applies, roughly $130, confirm), name a **registered agent** (a paid service can be your registered agent so your home address stays private), get a free **EIN** from the IRS, and open a business bank account. A free advisor at the Wisconsin SBDC or an accountant can walk you through it.
- **Keep your name private in practice:** domain WHOIS privacy (free at Cloudflare, Porkbun and Namecheap), a business email like hello@yourdomain, a mailbox or registered-agent address for the mailing address that email rules require, and your GitHub username is part of the web address until you connect your own domain.
- **When you hire:** have each person sign a contractor or employee agreement with the company, and check the rules on who counts as a contractor vs. an employee.
