# Design brief - Corner Chair (SAMPLE, a pretend barbershop)

> This is a sample to show the system. The business, phone and address are made up. A real brief is written after meeting the owner.

## Customers
Men and boys from the blocks around the shop: regulars who come every two or three weeks, dads bringing kids on a Saturday, and people on a lunch break who want a quick clean-up. They find a shop by walking past it or asking a friend, then they look up the hours and the phone number on their phone. Most of them are standing on a sidewalk when they do it.

## Neighborhood
Northwest side of Milwaukee, 53218: corner storefronts, bus stops, bungalows and duplexes. A barbershop here is a landmark people give directions by ("past the shop with the red sign").

## Vibe
Old-school corner shop. Loud sign, clean floor, a TV on in the corner. Friendly, no fuss, no fancy words.

## References
- A hand-lettered barbershop window: cream letters, a red outline, black glass behind it.
- A baseball ticket stub from the 1970s: tear-off edge, big numbers, one color of ink.
- A black-and-white checkerboard tile floor with a red leather chair on it.

## Palette
Cream (#F3EBDD) from the window lettering and the paper of the ticket. Near-black (#17140F) from the glass and the tile. Barber-pole red (#B3122B) from the sign outline and the chair. Only three colors, because a hand-painted sign only has three.

## Type
Alfa Slab One for the name and headings: a fat slab serif, the kind painted on shop windows and used on old posters. Work Sans for everything else because it is plain, readable on a phone in sunlight and does not compete with the slab.

## Layout
"Poster": a type-only hero. The name is stacked huge, like a poster in the window, with the Call button right under it. No photo is needed to look finished. Order: hero, hours, services, about, photos, contact. Hours come first because "when are you open?" is the first question.

## Signature
The hours are a ticket stub: dark stub, notches cut into the sides, a dashed tear line, slightly tilted. Today's row lights up.

## Voice
Short sentences. Say what they do and where. No claims we cannot prove (no "best in town").

## TO GET FROM OWNER
- Opening hours, photos, what the shop is known for, walk-ins or appointments, parking, payment types, languages spoken.
