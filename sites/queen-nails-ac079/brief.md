# Design brief - Queen Nails

> Built from public information only (name, address, phone, and a few public directory listings). We have not met the owner or seen the shop yet, so anything marked as a first guess must be confirmed at the visit.

## Customers
Women and girls booking nails for work, a party or a regular two-week refill; people looking for specific styles (extra-long, dip, Russian manicure) who want to see the list before they call. They use their phone to find the address, the hours and whether the shop does what they want.

## Neighborhood
West Capitol Drive near N 76th Street, in a small shopping strip on the northwest side. Parking in the lot out front.

## Vibe
Polished and personal. Like a salon price card: tidy, feminine, a little proud.

## References
- A printed salon service menu on heavy cream paper.
- A fashion magazine masthead with a thin double rule under it.
- A row of raspberry and blush nail polish bottles on a shelf.

## Palette
Blush paper (#FDF3F1), raspberry (#B8325A) from nail polish, and deep wine ink (#2E1B20) so text stays readable. A first guess: we have not seen their sign or colors.

## Type
Cormorant Garamond for headings: a refined, high-contrast serif with a beautiful italic, like a magazine title. Jost for body text: clean and geometric, easy to read on a phone.

## Layout
Editorial: a magazine page. The name sits large in italic serif under a double rule, with the services as an index list. Order: services, hours, about, facts, photos, contact. People choose a nail shop by what it offers, so the service list comes first.

## Signature
A double-rule masthead with a small folio line (type and city) above the name, like the top of a magazine page.

## Voice
Warm, brief, precise. Name the styles the way customers ask for them.

## TO GET FROM OWNER
- Confirm the service list (it comes from a directory), hours, suite number, prices if they want them shown, photos of their work (with permission), booking by phone or walk-in, Facebook page.
